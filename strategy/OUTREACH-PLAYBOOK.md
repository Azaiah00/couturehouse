# Couture House Salon Outreach Playbook

## Objective

Build genuine visibility among qualified salon owners and stylists, then create permission-based conversations around a clear digital problem. The goal is not to pitch everyone Couture House follows.

## List-building workflow

### Step 1: Pick one market and segment

Start with one combination for two weeks:

- Nashville natural-hair salons.
- Oakland locticians and loc salons.
- Existing client/referral networks.

Then test one expansion market, such as Atlanta, only after the first message produces replies.

### Step 2: Find candidate businesses

Use public business information from:

- Google Maps.
- Instagram and TikTok search/location tags.
- Public booking directories.
- Black-business directories.
- Cosmetology schools and salon-suite directories.
- Client collaborators and event pages.

Do not scrape private data, buy an unverified list, or collect personal information unrelated to the business.

### Step 3: Verify the fit

For each prospect, record:

- Business name and city.
- Owner/stylist name if publicly listed for business contact.
- Instagram/TikTok.
- Review count/rating.
- Booking platform.
- Website status.
- Specific strength.
- Specific digital gap.
- Buying signal.
- Score.
- Last interaction and next step.

### Step 4: Engage before pitching

Good engagement:

- A specific comment about a transformation, technique, education post, new service, or business milestone.
- A Story reply that asks a useful question.
- Sharing a post with clear credit when relevant.
- A thoughtful answer when the owner asks a business question.

Bad engagement:

- “Love this” repeated across accounts.
- Criticizing the website publicly.
- Following and immediately sending a generic pitch.
- Pretending to be a potential salon client.

## Message principles

- Lead with an observation, not a résumé.
- Acknowledge what is already working.
- Name one business consequence.
- Ask permission before sending a teardown.
- Keep the first message under roughly 70 words.
- Never promise rankings, followers, bookings, or revenue.
- Do not use fake urgency.

## Outreach scripts

### 1. After a real interaction

> Hey [Name]—your [specific service/transformation/post] stood out, especially [specific detail]. I noticed your social and reviews already do a lot of trust-building, but the path from discovery to [booking/service education/products] could be carrying more of that story. Would you be open to a short three-point website/booking audit? No pitch required.

### 2. No website, booking link only

> Hey [Name]—you have the pieces most businesses are still trying to build: strong work, active booking, and real client proof. Right now they live across social, reviews, and [booking platform]. I can show you how those pieces could become one owned digital home without replacing the booking tool that already works. Want the 90-second teardown?

### 3. Outdated website

> Hi [Name]—I found [Business] through [source] and your [specific strength] is clear. On mobile, the current site makes [specific task] harder than the social presence suggests it should be. I sketched three practical fixes that would preserve the brand and shorten the booking path. May I send them?

### 4. Product/Shopify opportunity

> Your clients already ask about [aftercare/product/topic] in the comments. That looks like a real opportunity to connect the service experience to products, kits, or education without turning the brand into a generic store. I have a simple “chair to checkout” concept for [Business]. Would it be useful to see?

### 5. Multi-stylist booking/portal opportunity

> I noticed [Business] has [number/type] stylists and several service paths. The current booking link asks a new client to know the right provider before the site has helped them choose. We build branded routing and client portals around existing salon operations. Can I send a quick example based on your setup?

### 6. Referral request to an existing client

> We’re focusing Couture House more deeply on Black-owned salons and stylists whose online presence has outgrown a booking link or older website. You’ve seen how we work. Is there one owner in your circle who has strong work and reviews but needs a better digital home? A warm introduction would mean a lot; I’ll lead with value and keep it respectful.

### 7. Partnership outreach

> Hi [Name]—Couture House builds websites, booking experiences, Shopify stores, and content systems for Black-owned salons and stylists. Your work in [photography/coaching/education/salon suites] reaches the same owners at a different point in their growth. I’d like to explore one genuinely useful resource or live session together—not a generic cross-promo. Open to a short conversation?

### 8. Follow-up one

> Circling back with one useful thought: [specific observation/fix in one sentence]. If the timing is not right, no problem—I’m happy to send the scorecard so you can review the digital presence internally.

### 9. Follow-up two / close the loop

> I’ll close the loop after this. The invitation for a short audit stays open, and I’ll keep following the work. If [website/booking/products] becomes a priority later, you can reply here with `CROWN`.

## Personalized audit format

Keep it to 90 seconds or one page:

1. **Strength:** What already creates trust.
2. **Friction:** One observable issue, shown on mobile.
3. **Opportunity:** One concrete improvement linked to bookings, products, or owner time.
4. **Proof:** A relevant Couture House example.
5. **Next step:** Scorecard or fit call.

Do not provide a complete unpaid strategy. The audit should demonstrate judgment and identify the next conversation.

## Discovery-call framework

### First five minutes

- Why now?
- What changed in the business?
- What do clients currently do before they book?

### Business model

- Highest-value services.
- Average ticket or useful range.
- Number of stylists/locations.
- Booking platform and payment/deposit rules.
- Product, education, membership, or gift-card revenue.

### Current funnel

- Where new clients come from.
- Repetitive questions in DMs.
- Website and booking problems.
- Review and rebooking process.
- What is measured now.

### Fit

- Decision makers.
- Desired launch timing.
- Comfortable investment range.
- Available photography/content.
- Need for website, portal, Shopify, content, or automation.

### Close

- Reflect the problem in business language.
- Recommend the smallest sufficient offer.
- Agree on next step and date.

## Qualification rule

Do not send a full proposal when:

- The decision maker is absent.
- The business problem is unclear.
- There is no credible budget/timing.
- The requested custom portal has no defined workflow.
- The prospect expects guaranteed growth.

Offer paid discovery or the scorecard instead.

## Ethical boundary

The target market’s current website is not evidence that the owner lacks business skill. Many successful stylists intentionally prioritize client work, social content, or booking operations. Couture House’s language should respect what they have already built and position the website as the next owned asset—not a rescue.
