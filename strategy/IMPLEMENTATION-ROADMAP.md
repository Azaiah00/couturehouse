# Couture House Implementation Roadmap

## Operating principle

Build the measurement and offer before scaling content or outreach. Attention is only useful when the website can identify, qualify, and convert the right salon owner.

## Phase 0 — Decisions and baseline (days 1–3)

Owner decisions:

- Confirm whether Couture House is nationwide remote, locally focused, or hybrid.
- Confirm Google Business Profile eligibility based on real in-person customer contact.
- Choose one primary offer to sell for 90 days.
- Set true minimum engagement and monthly project capacity.
- Confirm TikTok handle and account type.
- Confirm which clients permit logos, screenshots, testimonials, and results.

Baseline capture:

- Current Instagram and TikTok followers.
- Last 90 days of reach, profile visits, link clicks, and DMs.
- Current website sessions and leads, if analytics exists.
- Number of monthly discovery calls, proposals, closed clients, average project value, and lead source.
- Current reviews/testimonials.

## Phase 1 — Foundation (weeks 1–4)

### Week 1: Offer, measurement, and lead operations

- Finalize Digital Home Sprint scope, exclusions, timeline, and starting price.
- Create CRM stages:
  - Identified.
  - Qualified.
  - Engaged.
  - Audit requested.
  - Call booked.
  - Proposal sent.
  - Won.
  - Nurture.
  - Lost with reason.
- Install GA4 and define key events.
- Verify Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
- Create UTM naming standard.
- Connect project inquiries to CRM and email notifications.
- Add call scheduling.

**Definition of done**

- Every lead can be traced to a source.
- Form and calendar conversions are tested.
- Weekly reporting has an owner.

### Week 2: Search and trust foundation

- Add canonical metadata.
- Add Organization/ProfessionalService schema.
- Redirect `/for-brands`.
- Submit sitemap and request reindexing.
- Create Clutch profile.
- Start ByBlack application if eligible.
- Start Shopify Partner profile/training.
- Collect five review/testimonial requests from completed clients.

**Definition of done**

- Old URL resolves to a relevant current page.
- Search engines have a verified property and current sitemap.
- At least three testimonial conversations are active.

### Week 3: Lead magnet and first proof page

- Build Salon Digital Presence Scorecard.
- Create result-page recommendations.
- Add audit CTA to homepage, footer, and social bios.
- Publish Beverly’s case study.
- Record one client quote or short interview.

**Definition of done**

- A cold salon owner can receive useful value without completing the full project brief.
- Scorecard completion triggers CRM and analytics events.

### Week 4: First commercial landing page

- Publish `/salon-website-design`.
- Add supported booking platforms and relevant FAQs.
- Add a minimum engagement or starting-price signal.
- Publish the first three social posts and first email.

**Definition of done**

- Search/social visitors have one page that directly matches their problem.
- Page includes proof, process, price qualification, and conversion tracking.

## Phase 2 — Demand engine (weeks 5–12)

### Website

- Publish one high-value page or case study each week.
- Prioritize:
  1. Salon booking portals.
  2. Magic Coils case study.
  3. Hair stylist website design.
  4. Salon redesign.
  5. Shopify for hair and beauty.
  6. Dreadlocks Salon case study.
  7. Content creation.
  8. Pricing/process.

### Social

- Publish the 12-week calendar.
- Use one recurring series name and visual treatment.
- Record at least four posts in one monthly batch.
- Repurpose each weekly topic into a website article, email, and sales talking point.
- Use Instagram Collab posts with clients when they approve.

### Prospecting

Weekly:

- Research 50 qualified accounts.
- Add 30 prospects scoring 70+.
- Create 15 meaningful public interactions.
- Send 10 permission-based personalized messages.
- Offer three to five short audits to the best-fit prospects.
- Follow up twice, with value, then move to nurture.

### Partnerships

- Contact five complementary partners per month.
- Offer one co-created workshop, checklist, or live audit.
- Ask existing salon clients for introductions to two peers.

### Review

Every Friday:

- Which content created qualified conversations?
- Which page created audits or calls?
- Which prospect source produced the highest fit?
- Which objection appeared in calls?
- What should be added to the site or next week’s content?

## Phase 3 — Scale (months 4–6)

- Publish four additional case studies with measured outcomes.
- Build salon automation and digital-growth service pages.
- Launch one quarterly webinar or live audit.
- Test a small retargeting campaign only after conversion tracking works.
- Test high-intent Google Ads for the strongest service page.
- Build a referral agreement and partner kit.
- Add email nurture:
  - Scorecard delivery.
  - One quick win.
  - Case study.
  - Common objection.
  - Fit-call invitation.
- Create a paid strategy option for businesses that are not ready for a full build.
- Productize care/optimization retainers for every new website.

## Phase 4 — Authority (months 7–12)

- Publish original research: survey 50–100 salon owners about websites, booking tools, social dependence, and product sales.
- Release a “State of the Black Salon Digital Experience” report.
- Pitch the findings to beauty media, Black-business media, podcasts, and local business press.
- Create a founder/team profile with Person schema and demonstrated experience.
- Publish a booking-portal demo or small interactive tool.
- Build a searchable library of booking-platform integration guides.
- Pursue conference panels, cosmetology-school education, and salon-owner workshops.
- Expand paid acquisition only when organic/outreach close rates and delivery capacity are stable.

## Lead-response standards

- Acknowledge qualified inquiries within one business day.
- Send a useful observation before asking for a call.
- Use a 20-minute fit call before a long proposal.
- Send proposal within two business days of a qualified call.
- Follow up on days 2, 7, and 14 with a relevant answer, proof point, or resource.
- Record lost reasons: price, timing, fit, trust, internal delay, no response, or chose competitor.

## Responsibility map

| Function | Required owner |
|---|---|
| Strategy and weekly review | Founder/marketing lead |
| Content filming | Founder plus creative lead |
| Editing and distribution | Content producer |
| Prospect research and CRM | Sales/operations |
| Personalized audits | Founder or senior strategist |
| Website/SEO implementation | Development lead |
| Analytics and attribution | Development/marketing operations |
| Client proof collection | Account/project lead |

One person may hold several roles initially, but every task needs a named owner and deadline.

## Budget priorities

If budget is limited, fund in this order:

1. Analytics, CRM, and conversion flow.
2. Case studies and client proof.
3. Service landing pages.
4. Consistent founder-led content.
5. Email and review system.
6. Retargeting.
7. High-intent search ads.
8. Broader awareness ads.

Do not buy followers, engagement, bulk directory links, or generic blog packages.

## Risks and controls

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Beautiful content attracts designers, not buyers | Speak to bookings, reviews, staff, services, revenue, and owner time |
| Outreach feels predatory | Compliment real strengths; request permission before an audit; never publicly shame a prospect |
| Niche feels too narrow | Keep broader capability visible, but make salon/stylist acquisition the 90-day focus |
| Custom portals become unprofitable | Require discovery, document integrations, and reuse stable components |
| Claims cannot be proven | Publish methodology, period, source, and client permission |
| Too many offers confuse buyers | Lead with one Digital Home Sprint; introduce add-ons after diagnosis |
| Google Business Profile suspension | Create one only if the business meets face-to-face eligibility |
| Content cadence burns out | Batch monthly; reuse one idea across channels; keep three posts/week |
| Traffic rises but leads do not | Review offer, proof, CTA, form friction, and lead quality before increasing spend |

## 90-day success definition

At day 90 Couture House should have:

- A measurable funnel from content/search to call and sale.
- One clearly productized salon website offer.
- A low-friction scorecard/audit.
- At least three full salon/hair case studies.
- Five or more usable client testimonials/reviews.
- Four to six dedicated commercial service pages.
- A repeatable weekly social and prospecting routine.
- At least 12 qualified discovery calls generated during the quarter.
- Clear evidence about which message, city, service, and channel deserve the next investment.
