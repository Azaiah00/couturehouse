# Couture House Discoverability and Demand Strategy

Prepared: July 24, 2026  
Primary market: Black-owned hair salons, salon owners, independent stylists, locticians, natural-hair specialists, and textured-hair brands in the United States  
Working assumption: Couture House serves clients remotely nationwide. Nashville and Oakland should be the first proof-led markets because the portfolio already includes work in those cities.

## Executive decision

Couture House should own a narrower category:

> The digital growth studio for Black-owned salons and stylists who have built demand on social media but need a website, booking experience, store, and content system that can grow with the business.

The strongest opportunity is not to compete as another general web-design agency. It is to connect four things that competitors usually sell separately:

1. A culturally fluent, editorial website.
2. A booking journey or custom client portal.
3. Shopify commerce for products, gift cards, and education.
4. Social content and automations that turn attention into bookings and sales.

The core promise:

> Turn the audience and reputation you already built into a digital home that gets found, gets trusted, and gets booked.

## What the live-site audit found

### Existing strengths

- The visual identity is distinctive and premium.
- The home page already states “Digital atelier for hair, beauty & culture.”
- The portfolio includes highly relevant salon and textured-hair work: Magic Coils, Beverly’s of Nashville, The Dreadlocks Salon, and All Things Locs.
- The studio has a credible point of view about Black-owned beauty businesses.
- Services extend beyond websites into apps, portals, automations, content, e-commerce, motion, and music.
- The site is mobile responsive and its large media is now deferred appropriately.

### Discoverability and conversion gaps

- Only five URLs are in the sitemap. There are no dedicated, indexable pages for the services salon owners search for.
- Portfolio items live inside broad Work pages instead of having individual case-study URLs.
- The main routes contain no JSON-LD structured data.
- Canonical links were not present in the rendered metadata reviewed.
- Google is still showing Couture House’s older retail-brand positioning in some results, including an obsolete `/for-brands` result that now returns a 404.
- The site has no searchable resource library or lead magnet.
- The homepage has strong creative language but no low-friction “free audit” offer.
- There are no visible client testimonials, review count, before/after metrics, starting prices, or typical timelines.
- Instagram is linked; TikTok is not linked.
- No Google Analytics, Google Search Console verification, Meta Pixel, TikTok Pixel, or CRM integration was found in the repository review.
- The inquiry form posts to a third-party endpoint. It does not currently show a calendar or route leads into a visible CRM.

## Ideal customer profile

### Primary segment A: established independent stylist

- Specializes in natural hair, locs, braids, silk presses, extensions, color, wigs, or healthy-hair services.
- Has 2,000–50,000 social followers or strong engagement.
- Posts at least twice monthly.
- Uses GlossGenius, StyleSeat, Square, Booksy, Vagaro, Fresha, Acuity, or another booking platform.
- Has at least 20 public reviews with a strong average rating.
- Has no domain, uses only a booking link, or has an outdated/slow/template website.
- Average ticket is high enough that one to three incremental clients can justify a monthly marketing investment.

### Primary segment B: salon owner

- Has two or more stylists, multiple service categories, or multiple locations.
- Has a strong social presence and active booking system.
- Needs stylist profiles, service-specific pages, review capture, local SEO, deposits, client routing, or operational automation.
- Has products, classes, memberships, gift cards, or a future retail opportunity.

### Primary segment C: hair-care or salon-product brand

- Has an established product, salon distribution, or active social audience.
- Needs Shopify, product storytelling, subscriptions, wholesale inquiry flows, campaign content, or email automation.

### Disqualifiers

- No recent business activity.
- No evidence of paying clients or reviews.
- No current booking mechanism.
- Only wants a logo or one-off social post at commodity pricing.
- Cannot provide content, approvals, or access required to launch.
- Requests guaranteed rankings, followers, or revenue.

## Prospect score

Score every prospect before following or contacting them.

| Category | Points | Scoring rule |
|---|---:|---|
| Niche fit | 20 | Black-owned or clearly serves textured-hair clients; services match Couture House expertise |
| Social proof | 15 | Active Instagram/TikTok, recent posting, genuine engagement |
| Market proof | 15 | Reviews, press, awards, years in business, or visible client demand |
| Booking readiness | 10 | Active booking platform and clear service menu |
| Website pain | 25 | No site, broken link, template booking page only, outdated design, poor mobile UX, no SEO pages |
| Buying signals | 15 | Hiring, expanding, adding products/staff/location, rebranding, launching education, or recently asking about growth |
| **Priority threshold** | **70+** | Add to active outreach list |

Do not use follower count alone. A stylist with 3,000 followers, 100 reviews, and a $250 average service can be a better lead than an account with 50,000 passive followers.

## Productized offer ladder

Suggested price bands should be validated against Couture House’s delivery costs and capacity before publication.

### 1. Salon Digital Presence Audit

- Free self-assessment or short personalized video.
- Reviews mobile experience, search visibility, booking friction, trust, and commerce opportunity.
- CTA: book a 20-minute fit call.

### 2. Digital Home Sprint — suggested $3,500–$6,000

- Strategy and conversion-focused site.
- Booking integration.
- Services, portfolio, reviews, FAQ, local-search foundation.
- Mobile optimization and analytics.
- Best for an established independent stylist or suite owner.

### 3. Salon Growth Site — suggested $6,000–$12,000

- Everything in Digital Home Sprint.
- Individual services and stylist pages.
- Review system, local SEO, email capture, content migration, and launch campaign.
- Best for multi-stylist salons.

### 4. Custom Booking Portal — suggested $8,000–$20,000+

- Branded booking and client experience.
- Staff/service routing, deposits, forms, reminders, client dashboard, reporting, or selected custom workflows.
- Build only the functions the current booking platform cannot handle; do not rebuild commodity scheduling without a clear business case.

### 5. Shopify Commerce Add-On — suggested $3,000–$8,000+

- Products, gift cards, education, memberships, bundles, or wholesale inquiries.
- Product photography direction, merchandising, email flows, and analytics.

### 6. Content and Growth Partnership — suggested $1,500–$4,000/month

- Short-form content strategy and production.
- Campaign concepts, reels, carousels, email, landing pages, and monthly reporting.
- Use Couture House’s original music as a brand asset and differentiator.

### 7. Care and Optimization — suggested $300–$900/month

- Hosting, content updates, analytics review, testing, SEO hygiene, and support.
- Creates recurring revenue and keeps client sites from becoming outdated again.

### Primary lead magnet

**The Salon Digital Presence Scorecard**

Five sections, scored out of 100:

- Discoverability: Google, AI search, and local presence.
- Trust: reviews, proof, policies, and credentials.
- Booking: number of steps and mobile friction.
- Brand: visual consistency and cultural fit.
- Growth: email capture, products, automation, and measurement.

Results should route people into one of three recommendations:

- Repair: outdated or broken foundation.
- Rebuild: credible business but weak site.
- Scale: strong site that needs search, commerce, content, or automation.

### Secondary lead magnets

- “Your Booking Link Is Not Your Website” guide.
- Salon website cost calculator.
- Mobile booking checklist.
- Shopify revenue-opportunity worksheet.
- 90-second personalized homepage teardown.

## Google Business Profile decision

Do not create a profile until eligibility is confirmed.

Google requires in-person customer contact during stated hours. An online-only agency is not eligible. A staffed office where clients can visit, or a real service-area business that travels to customers, can be eligible. Virtual offices and borrowed addresses are not.

If eligible:

- Use the real-world business name without keywords added.
- Choose the smallest accurate category set; likely primary category “Website designer” or “Marketing agency.”
- Hide the home address if Couture House travels to clients and does not receive them there.
- Add services, website, phone, hours, portfolio photos, and weekly updates.
- Request honest reviews through Google’s review link or QR code after project milestones.
- Never offer discounts, gifts, or other incentives for reviews.
- Respond to every review with specific, concise language.

If not eligible:

- Do not risk suspension by creating an online-only listing.
- Build review proof on the website and a free Clutch profile.
- Create a ByBlack profile/certification if Couture House meets its ownership criteria.
- Join the Shopify Partner Program and work toward directory eligibility.
- Use client Google reviews inside case studies only with permission and clear attribution.

## Non-negotiable measurement rule

Do not report “followers,” “reach,” or “traffic” alone. The weekly scorecard must connect:

`content → profile visit → website visit → audit → call → proposal → sale`

The primary business metric is qualified opportunities created. The secondary metric is revenue attributable to each source.
