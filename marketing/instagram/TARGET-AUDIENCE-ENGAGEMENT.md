# Instagram Target-Audience Discovery and Engagement System

## The decision: should Couture House follow its target audience?

Yes—selectively.

Couture House should follow qualified salon owners, stylists, locticians, hair educators, suite owners and textured-hair brands when the account is genuinely relevant. Following is not the growth strategy by itself; it is one step in a human discovery and relationship system.

Do not follow hundreds of random accounts, use follow/unfollow tactics, buy followers, or automate repetitive engagement. Those behaviors create a weak audience and damage trust.

## Who qualifies for a follow

Follow an account when at least three of these are true:

- The owner or business clearly serves textured-hair clients.
- The account has posted real work within the last 30 days.
- There is evidence of paying clients, reviews, education, products or business activity.
- The account has a website, booking link or visible digital problem Couture House understands.
- The business is growing, hiring, launching, relocating, rebranding or adding services/products.
- The owner creates educational content or participates in salon-owner conversations.
- The business is connected to an existing Couture House client, partner or target city.

## Priority audiences

### Tier 1 — Buyers

- Multi-stylist Black-owned salon owners.
- Established independent stylists with specialized, higher-value services.
- Locticians and natural-hair specialists.
- Salon suite owners and salon educators.
- Textured-hair product brands with active commerce.

### Tier 2 — Referral partners

- Beauty photographers and videographers.
- Salon coaches and educators.
- Cosmetology schools.
- Salon suite operators.
- Beauty-business accountants, consultants and product formulators.
- Booking-system consultants and salon operations specialists.

### Tier 3 — Audience hubs

- Black beauty publications.
- Natural-hair communities.
- Local Black-business organizations.
- Hair shows, classes and industry events.
- Product distributors and professional beauty stores.

## Geography sequence

Start where Couture House already has proof:

1. Nashville — Beverly's.
2. Oakland / Bay Area — The Dreadlocks Salon and All Things Locs.
3. Charlotte — Photo Revival source work.
4. Atlanta — dense salon, stylist and Black-business market.
5. Washington, DC / DMV — existing business network and portfolio relevance.

Expand one city at a time after measuring which audience produces profile visits, DMs and calls.

## How to find the audience

### Instagram search

Search combinations of specialty + city:

- `loctician Nashville`
- `natural hair salon Oakland`
- `Black-owned salon Charlotte`
- `silk press Atlanta`
- `braider DMV`
- `salon suite owner Atlanta`
- `hair educator Bay Area`
- `textured hair brand`

Use Accounts, Places and recent relevant posts. Avoid treating hashtags as the only discovery source.

### Client-adjacent discovery

For every client collaborator:

1. Review the client's followers and commenters for salon owners, stylists and hair brands.
2. Review accounts the client tags in education, product, event and business posts.
3. Follow only qualified accounts.
4. Engage with their actual content before introducing Couture House.

Do not scrape private data or copy an entire follower list.

### Platform and directory discovery

Use public profiles from:

- Google Maps.
- StyleSeat, GlossGenius, Booksy, Vagaro, Square and Fresha.
- ByBlack and local Black-business directories.
- Cosmetology schools, salon suites and hair-show exhibitor lists.

Then verify that the Instagram account is active and business-focused.

## Daily 30-minute routine

### Ten minutes — discover

- Review one specialty/city search.
- Add five qualified accounts to the tracker.
- Follow two to five accounts that clearly fit.

### Ten minutes — engage

- Leave three specific comments that reference the actual post.
- Reply to one Story with a useful question or genuine observation.
- Save one post that reveals a business goal, launch or pain point.

### Ten minutes — respond

- Reply to all relevant comments and DMs on Couture House content.
- Visit accounts that saved, shared, followed or meaningfully commented where visible.
- Move warm conversations into the tracker.

No pitch is required during the first interaction.

## Weekly audience-building targets

| Action | Weekly target |
|---|---:|
| Qualified accounts researched | 25 |
| Selective follows | 15–25 |
| Specific public comments | 15 |
| Genuine Story replies | 5 |
| Client collaborator posts | 1 |
| Salon-owner conversations | 5 |
| `AUDIT` requests | 3 |

These are operating targets, not platform limits. Slow down if the activity becomes repetitive or the account begins attracting irrelevant followers.

## Engagement rules

Good comment:

> The way you explained the difference between maintenance and repair makes this much easier for a new client to understand.

Weak comment:

> Amazing 🔥 Check your DM.

Good Story reply:

> Is this a service you want new clients to book directly, or do you prefer a consultation first?

The goal is to be recognizable as a thoughtful specialist before asking for attention.

## Growth levers in priority order

1. Client collaboration posts.
2. Saveable salon-owner education.
3. Before/after website and content proof.
4. Useful comments on qualified accounts.
5. Story polls and question boxes.
6. Partner collaborations and Lives that do not require Fred on camera; use client interviews, screen-share audits or produced visual segments.
7. TikTok redistribution.
8. Paid promotion only after conversion tracking identifies a strong post and offer.

## Weekly review

Track:

- Follows gained from target accounts.
- Profile visits.
- Website taps.
- Saves and shares.
- Client collaborator reach.
- Qualified comments and DMs.
- Audit requests.
- Calls and closed work.

Stop any tactic that creates followers without salon-owner conversations, site visits or qualified inquiries.
